Fold-Paint Split Set, in leather.

A soft indigo lambskin overshirt whose lower half is 16 wide knife pleats, edge-stitched along every fold, and a wide stone suede trouser, lined to the knee, that pools on the floor. One hand-applied patina in rust and caramel starts on the shirt's pleats and runs on across the trouser's front pleats and back thighs. The pleats are closed while the colour goes on, so indigo and stone flash between the coloured faces when they move.

Overshirt flats · size M

Knife pleats all round the lower half: 4 per front half and 4 per back half = 16 pleats. Face PF ≈ 6.75 cm, depth PD 3 cm (intake 6 cm per pleat, 96 cm in all at M, so the panel is cut ≈ 224 cm round before pleating). Every fold turns TOWARD THE NEAREST SIDE SEAM: on the wearer's LEFT of CF the folds face the wearer's left side seam, on the wearer's RIGHT of CF they face the right side seam; the same on the back, so CB reads as a flat 7 cm panel between two pleats turning away from it (an inverted box look). First fold P0 = 3.5 cm from CF / CB, last fold ≈ 1.5 cm before the side seam. LEATHER: every fold is edge-stitched (1.5 mm from the fold) along its FULL length (PE = PL); lambskin takes no permanent pressed pleat, the stitch keeps the fold crisp. PD < PF, so the underlays do not overlap: ≈ 1.9 layers under one point of the pleat seam at M (wool version: 5). Allowances skived, glued and topstitched, no binding.

Trouser flats · size M

Pleats + crease in suede: suede holds no permanent pressed pleat or crease. The two front knife pleats are edge-stitched 1.5 mm from the fold for the first 12 cm below the waistband seam, then fall free; the crease is a soft press (cool iron through paper), not a sharp set. The nap is brushed after colouring.

How the colour is done

Route A, to be validated on leather strike-offs: the overshirt is cut in pale CRUST lambskin; the pleats are creased and closed; rust and caramel dyes go on first, the shapes are masked, then the indigo is airbrushed around them (the feathered edge is where the indigo fades into the caramel); a thin, flexible matte top coat seals it. The suede trouser is coloured with suede dyes airbrushed into the nap, then fixed, brushed and protected.

Route B (if route A fails on the strike-offs): the overshirt is cut from indigo crust lambskin drum-dyed at the tannery; rust + caramel are built up in thin layers of FLEXIBLE opaque leather pigment by airbrush (rust cores, caramel halos, feathered edges), then the same matte top coat. Simpler (no mask), but the coloured areas feel slightly more surface-coated. The trouser route does not change.

Strike-offs + leather tests

Strike-offsBoth routes (A crust + mask + indigo, B indigo crust + pigment) on 40 × 40 cm pleated lambskin, and the suede route on stone suede: shade, feathering, hand, nap, the pleat folds. Choose the route on these.
Rub fastness (Veslic)ISO 11640 on the coloured zones. Lambskin (top-coated): dry 150 cycles ≥ 4, wet 50 cycles ≥ 3. Suede: dry 50 cycles ≥ 3–4, wet 20 cycles ≥ 3 (grey scale for staining).
Flex resistanceISO 5402-1 (flexometer) on the coloured + top-coated lambskin: 20 000 cycles dry with no cracking, flaking or colour loss; also at the pleat fold.
Light fastnessISO 105-B02 (xenon arc): ≥ 4 on lambskin (indigo, rust, caramel), ≥ 3–4 on the suede.
Finish adhesion + perspirationISO 11644 finish adhesion ≥ 2 N/10 mm on lambskin; ISO 11641 perspiration ≥ 3 (colour transfer to the lining and skin).

How it is made

Overshirt · FP01L

  1. Leather: lambskin 0.7–0.8 mm, aniline / semi-aniline, soft temper, one dye lot per garment. Cut single-ply (hand or press knife), backbone vertical; pleated quarters from the best part of the skins.
  2. Silhouette: unchanged from the wool version: boxy unisex overshirt, hip length (A 70 cm at M), dropped shoulders (SH 58), relaxed chest (ease ≈ 20 cm), point collar on a stand, long sleeves with snap cuffs, back yoke. No pockets (open question).
  3. Knife pleats all round the lower half: 4 per front half and 4 per back half = 16 pleats. Face PF ≈ 6.75 cm, depth PD 3 cm (intake 6 cm per pleat, 96 cm in all at M, so the panel is cut ≈ 224 cm round before pleating). Every fold turns TOWARD THE NEAREST SIDE SEAM: on the wearer's LEFT of CF the folds face the wearer's left side seam, on the wearer's RIGHT of CF they face the right side seam; the same on the back, so CB reads as a flat 7 cm panel between two pleats turning away from it (an inverted box look). First fold P0 = 3.5 cm from CF / CB, last fold ≈ 1.5 cm before the side seam. LEATHER: every fold is edge-stitched (1.5 mm from the fold) along its FULL length (PE = PL); lambskin takes no permanent pressed pleat, the stitch keeps the fold crisp. PD < PF, so the underlays do not overlap: ≈ 1.9 layers under one point of the pleat seam at M (wool version: 5). Allowances skived, glued and topstitched, no binding.
  4. Pleat seam: level all round at PS = 33 cm below HPS at M. Pleats folded, edge-stitched full length and cool-pressed through paper first; the panel joined to the upper body, allowances skived, glued UP, topstitched 6 mm. The front plackets lie over it.
  5. Skin joins: a pleated quarter needs more length than one skin; joins are plain 1 cm, glued open and placed in the inner (under) fold of a pleat, where they never show.
  6. THE COLOUR is ANIEZE hand-applied patina on the finished FRONT and BACK UNITS (upper body + pleated panel joined, yoke not on yet), pleats closed and CLIPPED, never tacked (needle holes stay in leather). Only the pleat faces take colour; the underlays stay base colour and flash when the folds open.
  7. Colour route A (recommended): crust lambskin → rust + caramel shapes in penetrating dye → mask the shapes → airbrush the indigo base around them, blending the feathered edges → dry → thin flexible water-based top coat by spray → cure 24 h. Route B: indigo crust + thin opaque pigment layers by airbrush, sealed.
  8. Front: separate full-length plackets PW 3.5 cm, wearer's LEFT over right; 6 snaps on the front: 1 on the collar stand (12.5 mm) + 5 on the placket (15 mm) at S1 + k·SN. Fusible patch behind every snap.
  9. Back: lambskin outer yoke and cupro inner yoke to YK at CB; hanger loop under the collar. The back body below the yoke carries the colour, the yoke does not.
  10. Collar: point collar on a stand, leather fusible, allowances skived, edge-stitched 1.5 mm. Stand snap 12.5 mm.
  11. Sleeves: one-piece, set into the dropped armhole, seam glued and topstitched; 2 pleats into a 6 cm two-piece cuff, 14 cm tower placket; 1 snap per cuff (15 mm).
  12. Seams: plain 1 cm, glued open, topstitched 3 mm each side; upper body and sleeves half-lined in cupro; the pleated lower half unlined so the pleats swing. Skive every allowance that folds or crosses another.
  13. Hem: the pleated panel hem turned 2 cm, glued and topstitched before pleating, skived at every fold so it folds crisply.
  14. Labels: main woven label (real ANIEZE logo vector, 7.5 × 4.2 cm) on the cupro inner yoke at CB, top edge 2 cm below the collar seam; size label under it; care + composition in the lining at the wearer's LEFT side seam.
  15. Machinery: walking-foot or needle-feed lockstitch, leather-point needles, 3.5 mm stitch (topstitch 4 mm); skiving machine; roller or Teflon foot; snap press with the maker's dies. Test every seam and the snaps on coloured + coated leather first.
  16. Trouser · FP02L

  1. Leather: lamb suede 0.6–0.7 mm (thin + soft), drum-dyed through in warm stone, one dye lot per pair, nap running DOWN on every panel. Cut single-ply, crease on the backbone line where possible.
  2. Fit: unchanged from the wool version: very wide, high rise, unisex (house rule: wide, high-rise, pooling on the floor, nothing tied at the leg). Inseam 84 cm at M (174 cm, worn to the floor): body crotch height ≈ 80 cm; a wide high-rise trouser hangs its crotch ≈ 2 cm below the body crotch, leaving ≈ 78 cm to the floor barefoot; + ≈ 2 cm shoe sole = 80 cm in shoes; + ≈ 4 cm of pooled excess folding on the floor = 84 cm. Graded +1 cm per size with the crotch height, so every size pools the same ≈ 4 cm (house rule: wide, high-rise, pooling, nothing tied at the leg).
  3. Leg panels: suede skins are too short for one-piece legs, so each leg is an upper and a lower panel joined at KJ = 58 cm below the waistband seam (above the knee), lapped, glued and double topstitched: one level line all round that reads as a design seam.
  4. Front: two deep knife pleats per leg at the waistband seam, folds toward the SIDE seam (P1 on the crease line, intake 6 cm; P2 4 cm further out, intake 4.5 cm), edge-stitched for 12 cm, then free. Pleats + crease in suede: suede holds no permanent pressed pleat or crease. The two front knife pleats are edge-stitched 1.5 mm from the fold for the first 12 cm below the waistband seam, then fall free; the crease is a soft press (cool iron through paper), not a sharp set. The nap is brushed after colouring.
  5. THE COLOUR: rust + caramel suede dye, airbrushed in thin passes over the closed (clipped) pleats of the front legs from the waistband seam, fading out between mid-thigh and the knee, and on the back thighs; nap brushed after drying; then the repellent.
  6. The legs are coloured on the registration template together with the overshirt's units, the same day and with the same mixes, so the composition continues from the shirt's pleats onto the trouser. Waistband, loops, fly shield and pocket facings stay plain.
  7. Slant front pockets: opening from SPX at the waistband seam to SPD down the side seam; stay-taped, edge turned and glued, edge-stitched + topstitched 6 mm; cotton-cupro bags.
  8. Fly: metal #5 zip, wearer's LEFT over right, J-stitch 3.8 cm. Waistband: 4.5 cm, suede outer, cupro-covered curtain facing, split at CB; hook + bar + inside button.
  9. Belt loops: 6, suede, edges turned and glued, edge-stitched, caught in the waistband seam and bartacked.
  10. Back: one dart per side into the jet; two jetted pockets (2 × 5 mm jets), no flap or button. The back colour starts ≥ 5 cm below the jets.
  11. Lining: Bemberg cupro, front and back, waistband seam to the knee (LLF), free hem. It keeps the suede off the skin and protects the legs from any colour transfer.
  12. Hem: turned 3 cm and glued (no visible stitch), hidden hem guard tape inside the back; the hem pools ≈ 4 cm on the floor in soft folds, no turn-up.
  13. Labels: main woven label (real ANIEZE logo vector, 6 × 3.4 cm) on the waistband facing at CB; size + care in the lining at the wearer's LEFT side seam.
  14. Machinery: walking-foot or needle-feed lockstitch, leather-point needles, 3.5 mm stitch; skiving machine; Teflon or roller foot (suede marks easily); bartack; no steam press on suede.
  15.
