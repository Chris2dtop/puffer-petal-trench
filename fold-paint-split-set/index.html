Fold-Paint Split Set.

An indigo wool overshirt with a knife-pleated lower half, and a wide stone wool trouser that pools on the floor. One hand-painted composition in rust and caramel starts on the shirt's pleats and runs on across the trouser's front pleats and back thighs. The pleats are basted shut while painting, so indigo and stone flash between the painted faces when they move.

Overshirt flats · size M

Knife pleats all round the lower half: 9 per front half and 9 per back half = 36 pleats. Face PF ≈ 3 cm, depth PD 6 cm (intake 12 cm per pleat, 432 cm in all at M, so the panel is cut ≈ 560 cm round before pleating). Every fold turns TOWARD THE NEAREST SIDE SEAM: on the wearer's LEFT of CF the folds face the wearer's left side seam, on the wearer's RIGHT of CF they face the right side seam; the same on the back, so CB reads as a flat 7 cm panel between two pleats turning away from it (an inverted box look). First fold P0 = 3.5 cm from CF / CB, last fold ≈ 1.5 cm before the side seam. Each pleat is edge-stitched shut (1.5 mm from the fold) for the first PE = 2 cm below the pleat seam, then hangs free. Because PD > PF the underlays overlap: ≈ 5.0 layers sit under one point of the pleat seam at M, so the seam allowance is graded and Hong Kong bound.

Trouser flats · size M

Permanent pleats + crease: the two front knife pleats and the front and back creases get a CHEMICAL permanent set (monoethanolamine-sulphite type, Si-Ro-Set principle) at the pleating / finishing house, before painting; after painting, steaming and rinsing they are re-pressed and re-set. Steam alone gives a 'cohesive' set that water and steam release. To be validated on proto panels.

How the painting is done

Route A, to be validated on proto panels: pleat and chemically set the panels, baste the pleats shut, paint both pieces together on one template, steam-fix, rinse, release and re-set. The indigo overshirt is DISCHARGE-painted (rust and caramel acid dye alone would barely show on indigo); the stone trouser is painted directly with acid dye.

Fallback B (only if route A fails on the proto panels): paint the panels FLAT, steam-fix, rinse, then pleat and set. The composition then splits across pleat faces and underlays (the shapes look broken into stripes when the pleats close) and registration across the pleats is lost. Not the design intent.

1. Process risks to settle before buying anything

  1. Rust and caramel acid dye painted on an indigo or navy base will hardly show. Acid dye is transparent, so it reads as a muddy darkening on a dark ground. There are three ways around this:
    • (a) Accept a tonal effect. Only try this after a test.
    • (b) Use discharge painting. The base cloth must be dyed with dischargeable acid dyes. You then paint a discharge paste containing illuminating (discharge-resistant) acid dyes. Kemtex (UK) sells all three components: dischargeable acid dyes, illuminating acid dyes, and Decrolin discharge agent. Kemtex says Decrolin is preferred for wool because it is not alkaline, and has a technical sheet, DP35 "Discharge Printing of Wool Dyed with Acid Dyes". This means you must commission the base dyeing (from PFD/ecru cloth, or with a mill), because random stock indigo cloth will not be dischargeable.
    • (c) Use a lighter "indigo" base shade. The stone base is fine for rust and caramel.
  2. Order of operations. Wool pleats set with steam alone are a "cohesive set": water or steam can release them. A permanent pleat in wool needs a chemical setting agent, which is the Si-Ro-Set idea. In practice:
    • Paint, steam-fix and wash off the pleat panels flat first.
    • Then pleat them with a chemical-assisted set.
    • If you steam-fix a garment that is already pleated, expect the pleats to move. Trial both routes.
  3. The base cloth must be dyeable. Ask for:
    • no fluorocarbon or other water-repellent finish;
    • no heavy silicone softener;
    • no resin or anti-felt finish that blocks acid dye (or a declaration of what finish is applied). PFD (prepared for dyeing) or "ready-to-dye" cloth is ideal.
  4. Fibre blends. Polyester in a blend does not take acid dye, so those areas stay unpainted and the colour looks heathered. Nylon/polyamide and silk do take acid dye. Elastane is fine in small amounts.
  5. Thread and trims. Polyester core-spun thread (for example Coats Epic) will not take acid dye. Neither will polyester woven labels or polyester interlinings. If paint crosses topstitching, choose the thread colour on purpose, or use silk or polyamide thread.
  6. Fusing and steaming. 30–60 minutes of saturated steam at about 100–104 °C can delaminate some fusibles. Paint and steam before fusing, or test fused panels through the full steam cycle.
  7. Wash-off decides the rubbing result (ISO 105-X12).
    • After steaming, rinse out the thickener and unfixed dye gently, at 40 °C or below with no agitation, to avoid felting.
    • Over-applied paint gives poor wet rubbing.
    • Milling acid dyes and 1:2 metal-complex dyes (Lanaset type) have better wet and light fastness than levelling acid dyes.

7. Briefing checklists

7a. Brief to the mill or stock platform

  • [ ] 100% wool (or wool plus a small amount of elastane or polyamide only). No polyester.
  • [ ] Gabardine or steep twill; state the twill direction you want.
  • [ ] Weight: overshirt 220–240 g/m², trousers 280–320 g/m². Ask for usable width and g per linear metre.
  • [ ] Base: PFD / ready-to-dye / ecru, or piece-dyed shade "indigo" and "stone" with lab-dip approval.
  • [ ] If painting on indigo: can the base be dyed with dischargeable acid dyes? Ask for the dye class.
  • [ ] Finishing declaration: no water/stain repellent, no silicone, no resin. State the anti-felt treatment if any. Is it decatised, and to what grade (KD or semi)?
  • [ ] Shrinkage to steam (e.g. IWS/TM or a steam test). Wool shrinks during 30–60 min steam fixation.
  • [ ] Fastness of the base colour to D01, X12, B02 and to steaming. The base must not bleed into the painted areas.
  • [ ] MOQ per colour, stock or repeatability, lead time, sample yardage (3–5 m for painting and pleating trials), price per metre at 150 m and 800 m.
  • [ ] Certifications: RWS, OEKO-TEX, origin documents for export.

7b. Brief to the pleater

  • [ ] Fibre: 100% wool gabardine at the stated weights. Send the actual cloth for strike-offs.
  • [ ] Pleat type: knife pleats. Give depth (e.g. 15–25 mm), spacing, direction, and the length of the pleated zone (overshirt lower half; trouser front pleats).
  • [ ] Format: cut panels or fabric length? Pleat before or after painting? (Recommended: paint, steam and wash first, then pleat.)
  • [ ] Setting method: paper moulds or machine, steam conditions, chemical setting agent (MEAS, bisulphite or other), and how the residue is neutralised and rinsed (it causes odour).
  • [ ] Any effect of setting chemistry or steam on painted colours. Ask for a test on painted swatches.
  • [ ] Durability: pleat retention after dry cleaning (D01) and steam pressing, and after a damp-cloth test.
  • [ ] Width take-up factor, so fabric can be ordered correctly (knife pleats typically take about 3×).
  • [ ] MOQ, price per panel or per metre, lead time, transit packaging that keeps pleats.

7c. Brief to the dye, paint or steam house

  • [ ] Dye class: milling acid or 1:2 metal-complex (Lanaset type), or Kemtex dischargeable/illuminating for indigo. Give shade standards for rust and caramel.
  • [ ] Paste recipe: thickener (alginate, locust bean gum or guar) at a stated %, urea, thiodiglycol or DEG, acid donor (ammonium sulphate or formic acid, pH 3–5), optional sodium chlorate.
  • [ ] Application: brush, sponge or stencil. Set the maximum paste load per m² (over-loading ruins wet rubbing).
  • [ ] Drying, then saturated steam 100–104 °C for 30–60 min (trial times). Protect with cotton or newsprint interleaves so colour does not transfer.
  • [ ] Wash-off: cold rinse, then ≤40 °C rinse with a wool-safe detergent, no agitation, flat dry. Check felting and shrinkage.
  • [ ] Order relative to fusing, pleating and sewing. Run steam tests on fused panels.
  • [ ] Lab tests: X12, D01, B02, E04, E07 on the steamed and washed swatches before bulk.
  • [ ] Capacity per day, cost per piece, rejects policy, photo standard for "hand-painted variation".

8. Biggest gaps and suggested next steps

  1. No Pakistani worsted gabardine source was confirmed. Plan to import:
    • Nona Source or Zerow for the 50-piece run;
    • Yünsa or Italian mills for the 300-piece run.
  2. No Pakistani pleater or wool steam-fix house was confirmed. Two options:
    • pleat in Europe (Omniapiega, a Paris pleater, or a UK pleater) and ship panels to Sialkot; or
    • set up a small steamer plus MEAS pleat-setting in Sialkot with Woolmark or Archroma technical support.
  3. Indigo plus rust/caramel acid paint is a colour-physics problem, not a sourcing one. Decide between a tonal effect and the discharge route before buying cloth.
  4. Labs: Tti (Lahore, with a Sialkot liaison office) covers D01. BV Lahore covers B02 and X12. SGS Karachi covers dry-cleaning and rubbing. Confirm current scopes on PNAC.

How it is made

Overshirt · FP01

  1. Cloth: 100 % wool gabardine / twill 220–240 g/m², piece-dyed deep indigo, decatised. Cut with the grain vertical; pleated panel quarters cut across the full width (pleats run with the warp).
  2. Silhouette: boxy unisex overshirt, hip length (A 70 cm at M), dropped shoulders (SH 58), relaxed chest (ease ≈ 20 cm), point collar on a stand, long sleeves with snap cuffs, back yoke. No pockets (open question).
  3. Knife pleats all round the lower half: 9 per front half and 9 per back half = 36 pleats. Face PF ≈ 3 cm, depth PD 6 cm (intake 12 cm per pleat, 432 cm in all at M, so the panel is cut ≈ 560 cm round before pleating). Every fold turns TOWARD THE NEAREST SIDE SEAM: on the wearer's LEFT of CF the folds face the wearer's left side seam, on the wearer's RIGHT of CF they face the right side seam; the same on the back, so CB reads as a flat 7 cm panel between two pleats turning away from it (an inverted box look). First fold P0 = 3.5 cm from CF / CB, last fold ≈ 1.5 cm before the side seam. Each pleat is edge-stitched shut (1.5 mm from the fold) for the first PE = 2 cm below the pleat seam, then hangs free. Because PD > PF the underlays overlap: ≈ 5.0 layers sit under one point of the pleat seam at M, so the seam allowance is graded and Hong Kong bound.
  4. Pleat seam: one level horizontal seam all round at PS = 33 cm below HPS at M (upper abdomen, ≈ 10 cm above the natural waist, as in the design image). Pleats are formed, basted and pressed first, the panel is joined to the upper body, allowances graded, Hong Kong bound, pressed UP and topstitched 6 mm on the upper body side. The front plackets are separate strips laid over it, so the seam stops at the placket.
  5. THE PAINT is DISCHARGE painting (indigo removed and replaced by rust / caramel) and goes on BEFORE the rest of the garment is made (see the painting process, to be validated on proto panels): paint units = front sub-assembly (upper front + pleated front panel joined at PS; the plackets go on after painting and stay plain indigo) and back sub-assembly (back body + pleated back panel joined at PS, yoke NOT on yet). Pleats stay basted shut while painting, so only the pleat faces take colour and the underlays stay indigo: when the pleats open, indigo flashes between the painted faces (the 'fold' in fold-paint). Sleeves, yoke, collar, stand, cuffs stay plain indigo.
  6. Sequence (to be validated on proto panels): cut → pleat the panels → chemical permanent set (MEA-sulphite type, Si-Ro-Set principle) → baste the pleats shut → join the paint units → discharge-paint on the registration template with the trouser panels → steam-fix → rinse / neutralise → release the basting → re-press / re-set the pleats → fuse the interfacings and finish the garment. Fallback B (paint flat, then pleat) breaks the composition differently (shapes split across faces and underlays) and is a fallback only.
  7. Front: separate full-length plackets PW 3.5 cm, wearer's LEFT over right, from the neck to the hem across the pleat seam; 6 snaps on the front: 1 on the collar stand (12.5 mm) + 5 on the placket (15 mm) at S1 + k·SN. The pleats start P0 = 3.5 cm from CF.
  8. Back: yoke (outer + inner) to YK at CB, back body sandwiched between the yokes, topstitched 1.5 mm; hanger loop under the collar. The back body below the yoke carries the paint, the yoke does not.
  9. Collar: point collar on a stand, both interfaced with woven fusible, edge-stitched 1.5 mm. Stand snap 12.5 mm.
  10. Sleeves: one-piece, set flat into the dropped armhole, flat-felled; 2 pleats into a 6 cm folded cuff with a 14 cm tower placket; 1 snap per cuff (15 mm).
  11. Seams: flat-felled 6 mm on the armholes, side and sleeve seams (unlined garment, clean inside). Pleat seam and pleated panel joins: Hong Kong bound with indigo cupro bias.
  12. Hem: the pleated panel hem is double-turned 1.5 cm and stitched before pleating (flat), so the hem folds crisply into each pleat.
  13. Labels: main woven label (real ANIEZE logo vector, 7.5 × 4.2 cm) on the inner yoke at CB, top edge 2 cm below the collar seam; size label under it; care + composition in the wearer's LEFT side seam, 10 cm above the pleat seam. Care in words FR/EN: DRY CLEAN ONLY · hand-painted, each piece unique.
  14. Machinery: single-needle lockstitch 3 mm stitch (topstitch 3.5 mm), felling machine or folder, steam press with pleat boards, snap press with the maker's dies. Test every seam, the pleat press and the snaps on a painted + steamed swatch first.
  15. Care wording (FR/EN): 100 % LAINE / WOOL · NETTOYAGE À SEC UNIQUEMENT / DRY CLEAN ONLY · Ne pas laver, ne pas blanchir, ne pas sécher en tambour / Do not wash, bleach or tumble dry · Repasser à la vapeur sur l'envers avec une pattemouille, sans écraser les plis / Steam iron on the reverse with a press cloth, do not flatten the pleats · Peint à la main : chaque pièce est unique, les variations de couleur et de forme font partie du design / Hand-painted: each piece is unique; variations in colour and shape are part of the design.

Trouser · FP02

  1. Cloth: 100 % wool gabardine 280–320 g/m², warm stone, no finish that blocks dye. Cut with the crease on the straight grain; all pieces of one pair from one lot.
  2. Fit: very wide, high rise, unisex (house rule: wide, high-rise, pooling on the floor, nothing tied at the leg). Inseam 84 cm at M (174 cm, worn to the floor): body crotch height ≈ 80 cm; a wide high-rise trouser hangs its crotch ≈ 2 cm below the body crotch, leaving ≈ 78 cm to the floor barefoot; + ≈ 2 cm shoe sole = 80 cm in shoes; + ≈ 4 cm of pooled excess folding on the floor = 84 cm. Graded +1 cm per size with the crotch height, so every size pools the same ≈ 4 cm (house rule: wide, high-rise, pooling, nothing tied at the leg).
